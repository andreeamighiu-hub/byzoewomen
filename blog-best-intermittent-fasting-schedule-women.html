The Best Intermittent Fasting Schedule for Women:
How Many Hours, Which Days.

Most fasting schedules tell you to fast 16 hours every day. For a woman, that is the wrong instruction — because the right fasting window changes across your menstrual cycle. Here is the schedule built for a 28-day body, not a 24-hour one.

By Andreea Mighiu · Zōē · byzoewomen.com

How many hours should a woman fast?

The number circulating online — fast 16 hours, eat for 8 — comes almost entirely from research conducted on men. Male hormones reset on a roughly 24-hour cycle, so a fixed daily fast suits male biology. Women run a 28-day hormonal cycle, and the body's tolerance for fasting changes week to week within it.

For most of the cycle, the evidence points to a moderate fast of 12 to 14 hours as the sustainable range for women. The window extends to 14 to 16 hours only in the follicular phase, when the body is most metabolically resilient. Fasting beyond 16 hours, or holding a long daily fast across every phase, is where reproductive hormones start to be affected — irregular cycles, worsening PMS and stalled fat loss being the early signs.

How many hours should a woman fast?
Most women do best with a 12 to 14 hour fast for the majority of the cycle, extending to 14 to 16 hours only in the follicular phase. Fasting longer than 16 hours, or fasting daily without regard to cycle phase, can raise cortisol and disrupt reproductive hormones. In the late luteal phase and menstruation, a 12 hour overnight fast is the maximum.

Why the schedule has to change across your cycle

Fasting is a cortisol stimulus. The body reads the absence of food as a potential energy shortage and raises cortisol to mobilize stored energy. In men, this response is roughly the same every day. In women, the same fast produces a measurably different cortisol response depending on cycle phase — lowest in the follicular phase when estrogen is high and cortisol sensitivity is low, highest in the late luteal phase when estrogen and progesterone are falling and cortisol sensitivity peaks.

This is the whole reason a single fixed schedule fails women. A fast that feels easy and works beautifully in week two becomes a source of anxiety, poor sleep and uncontrollable hunger in week four — not because your discipline changed, but because the hormonal environment did.

The best intermittent fasting schedule for women, phase by phase

Follicular phase (Days 6 to 13) — your fasting window. Insulin sensitivity is highest, estrogen is rising, cortisol sensitivity is lowest. A 14 to 16 hour fast — an eating window of roughly 10am to 6pm — is generally well tolerated and delivers real metabolic benefit. This is the phase where intermittent fasting does what its proponents promise.

Ovulatory phase (Days 14 to 16) — shorten it. Estrogen and testosterone peak, performance is at its highest. Fuel it. A 12 hour overnight fast maximum.

Early luteal phase (Days 17 to 22) — 12 hour maximum. Progesterone is rising and metabolism is climbing. The body needs more fuel, not less. A 12 hour overnight fast — stop eating at 8pm, eat at 8am — is the limit.

Late luteal phase (Days 23 to 28) — no fasting. Cortisol sensitivity is at its monthly peak and metabolic rate is elevated. Any fast here is a stress signal applied to the most stress-sensitive week of the cycle. It worsens PMS, disrupts sleep and promotes fat storage. Eat regularly, lean on complex carbohydrates and magnesium-rich foods.

Menstrual phase (Days 1 to 5) — no fasting. The body is replenishing iron and recovering. Adequate calories and anti-inflammatory foods matter far more than a fasting window here.

What is the best intermittent fasting schedule for women?
Fast 14 to 16 hours in the follicular phase (days 6 to 13), a 12 hour overnight fast through ovulation and early luteal (days 14 to 22), and no fasting in the late luteal phase (days 23 to 28) or during menstruation (days 1 to 5). This captures the benefits of fasting in the phases the body tolerates it and protects hormonal balance in the phases it does not.

What about cycle syncing if your periods are irregular?

If your cycle is irregular, start by anchoring the schedule to what you can observe. The follicular phase begins when your period ends; the luteal phase is the roughly two weeks before your next period. Even a rough map is better than a fixed daily fast applied blindly. Tracking your cycle for two to three months usually reveals enough of a pattern to sync to — and the act of tracking often surfaces the very irregularity that fasting may have been contributing to.

Is 16:8 fasting good for women?
16:8 works well for women in the follicular phase, where it is generally well tolerated and metabolically beneficial. Applied every day across the whole cycle — including the late luteal phase and menstruation — it becomes a daily cortisol stimulus that can disrupt the menstrual cycle, worsen sleep and stall fat loss. The schedule is not the problem; the timing is.
