Training & Hormones

Signs your training plan
isn't working with your hormones.

Sore for longer than usual. Hungrier than your plan accounts for. Wiped out by a session that used to feel easy. These are not signs you are lazy or inconsistent. The fast answer: they are signs your training plan is not accounting for your hormonal cycle — and at Zōē, this is the first thing we check with every new client, because it explains a lot of frustration that otherwise gets blamed on willpower. Below are the specific signs to watch for, and what each one is actually telling you.

By Andreea Mighiu  ·  Training & Hormones  ·  byzoewomen.com

The five signs worth paying attention to

Soreness that lingers well past the usual 24 to 48 hours. If a familiar workout suddenly leaves you sore for three or four days, your recovery capacity has shifted — not your fitness level.

Hunger that spikes without warning. A jump in appetite in the days before your period is a real physiological response to falling progesterone and rising metabolic demand, not a lack of discipline.

Sessions that feel disproportionately hard. The same weight, pace or class that felt manageable two weeks ago suddenly feeling much harder is a common luteal-phase experience tied to lower available fuel and higher cortisol sensitivity.

Sleep that gets worse right before your period. Elevated progesterone raises body temperature slightly, disrupting the temperature drop that normally initiates deep sleep — which compounds every other symptom on this list.

Motivation that seems to vanish for no reason. Falling estrogen reduces serotonin and dopamine sensitivity in the premenstrual days, which can look like a lack of willpower but is a measurable neurochemical shift.

What are the signs my workout plan isn't hormone-aware?
The main signs are: soreness lasting longer than usual, appetite changes your plan doesn't account for, sessions feeling harder than they should for no clear reason, worse sleep in the days before your period, and unexplained drops in motivation — all of which follow a predictable monthly pattern once you start tracking them.

What each sign is actually telling you

None of these signs mean something is wrong with you. They mean your hormonal cycle is doing exactly what it is supposed to do — and your training plan has not been told about it. Estrogen and progesterone are not a footnote to your fitness; they are actively shaping your recovery capacity, appetite regulation, sleep quality and motivation every single week.

These are not inconsistencies in your discipline. They are consistencies in your biology — once you know what to look for.

What to do once you notice the pattern

The first step is simply tracking which phase each sign shows up in. Once you see the pattern — that the soreness, the hunger, the harder sessions all cluster in the same window every month — you can start adjusting training volume and intensity ahead of time instead of reacting to it after the fact. This is the entire premise behind how Zōē structures every client's plan.

How do I know if my fatigue is normal or a sign of overtraining?
If fatigue follows a predictable monthly pattern tied to your luteal phase and resolves once your period starts, it is very likely hormonal, not overtraining. If fatigue is constant, worsening over multiple cycles, or accompanied by injury or illness, that pattern points toward genuine overtraining and is worth discussing with a healthcare provider.
