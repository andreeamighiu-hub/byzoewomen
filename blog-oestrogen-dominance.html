Estrogen Dominance in Women:
Symptoms, Causes and How to Fix It Naturally.

Estrogen dominance is one of the most searched hormonal terms in women's health — and one of the most misunderstood. It is not simply having too much estrogen. It is having too much estrogen relative to progesterone. Here is what that means, what causes it, and what actually works.

By Andreea Mighiu · Zōē · byzoewomen.com

What estrogen dominance actually means — the precise definition

Estrogen does not operate in isolation. Its effects on the body depend heavily on its ratio to progesterone. When estrogen is high and progesterone is adequate, most women feel well. When estrogen is high and progesterone is low — or when progesterone drops faster than estrogen at the end of the luteal phase — the relative dominance of estrogen produces a characteristic cluster of symptoms.

This can happen in three ways. Estrogen is genuinely elevated — from excess production, impaired clearance or external exposure. Progesterone is genuinely low — from insufficient ovulation, chronic stress or nutritional deficiency. Or both are occurring simultaneously.

What is estrogen dominance?
Estrogen dominance is a state in which estrogen is elevated relative to progesterone — either because estrogen is too high, progesterone is too low, or both. It disrupts the hormonal balance the female cycle depends on and produces a characteristic cluster of symptoms including heavy periods, PMS, mood changes, weight gain around the hips and thighs, breast tenderness and bloating.

The symptoms — and what each one means hormonally

Heavy or painful periods. Excess estrogen stimulates the endometrium — the uterine lining — to build beyond what is needed. When progesterone cannot adequately oppose this proliferation, the lining becomes thicker and the period heavier. The same imbalance increases prostaglandin production, which drives period pain.

Premenstrual mood symptoms. Excess estrogen relative to progesterone in the luteal phase disrupts the GABA and serotonin systems that progesterone normally supports. The emotional reactivity, anxiety and irritability of PMS are amplified significantly when estrogen dominance is present.

Weight gain around the hips, thighs and abdomen. Estrogen stimulates fat storage in specific depots — the hips, thighs and lower abdomen. Elevated estrogen also promotes water retention and can contribute to insulin resistance. This fat pattern is often described as feeling impossible to shift — and in an estrogen-dominant state it largely is, because the hormonal environment is actively promoting its storage.

Breast tenderness. One of the most consistent symptoms of elevated estrogen. Progesterone has a direct anti-proliferative effect on breast tissue — when it cannot adequately oppose estrogen, breast tissue becomes more sensitive and tender, particularly in the premenstrual week.

What are the symptoms of estrogen dominance?
Heavy or painful periods, premenstrual mood changes, weight gain around hips and thighs, breast tenderness, bloating in the second half of the cycle, low libido, fatigue, brain fog and irregular cycles. These symptoms cluster in the luteal phase when progesterone should be balancing estrogen but cannot do so adequately.

What causes it — the five main mechanisms

Impaired liver estrogen clearance. The liver is the primary route by which estrogen is metabolised and prepared for excretion. When liver function is compromised — by alcohol, poor nutrition, excess saturated fat or toxic load — estrogen is not cleared efficiently and recirculates. Alcohol is the most significant dietary contributor: even moderate drinking (1 to 2 units daily) measurably elevates circulating estrogen by reducing liver clearance capacity.

Source: Gavaler & Van Thiel, The association between moderate alcoholic beverage consumption and serum estradiol and testosterone levels in normal postmenopausal women — PubMed.

Poor gut microbiome health. The gut microbiome plays a direct role in estrogen metabolism through a collection of bacteria called the estrobolome. These bacteria produce beta-glucuronidase — an enzyme that can reactivate conjugated estrogen and return it to circulation rather than allowing it to be excreted. Poor gut health dysregulates this process, effectively recycling estrogen rather than eliminating it. This is why gut health is directly relevant to hormonal balance — not through a vague wellness connection but through a specific metabolic mechanism.

Chronic cortisol elevation. Cortisol and progesterone share the same receptor sites. Chronically elevated cortisol competes with progesterone for those receptors, effectively reducing progesterone's activity without changing its blood level. This is functional progesterone deficiency — a state in which progesterone appears normal on a blood test but cannot adequately oppose estrogen because cortisol is occupying its receptors. Read more about how cortisol disrupts the hormonal cycle.

Xenoestrogen exposure. Xenoestrogens are environmental compounds that mimic estrogen in the body — including BPA from plastics, phthalates from personal care products, parabens from cosmetics and certain pesticide residues. They bind to estrogen receptors and activate them without being actual estrogen, effectively adding to total oestrogenic load.

Excess body fat. Adipose tissue (body fat) converts androgens to estrogen through an enzyme called aromatase. Higher body fat increases aromatase activity and therefore estrogen production — which is why weight loss is both a consequence and a cause of estrogen dominance.

What causes estrogen dominance in women?
The five main mechanisms: impaired liver estrogen clearance (alcohol is the single biggest dietary contributor), poor gut microbiome health disrupting estrogen excretion, chronic cortisol competing with progesterone for receptor sites, xenoestrogen exposure from plastics and personal care products, and excess body fat increasing aromatase-driven estrogen production.

How to reduce estrogen dominance naturally — the evidence

Cruciferous vegetables and DIM. Broccoli, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts and kale contain indole-3-carbinol, which the liver converts to DIM (diindolylmethane). DIM supports the liver's estrogen metabolism pathways — specifically promoting conversion to less potent estrogen metabolites rather than more potent ones. Three to five servings per week of cruciferous vegetables, particularly in the follicular phase, directly supports healthy estrogen clearance. Read more in the cycle syncing diet guide.

Magnesium glycinate. Magnesium is a cofactor in phase two liver detoxification — the pathway responsible for conjugating estrogen before excretion. Magnesium deficiency (extremely common in women) directly impairs this process. At 375mg daily, magnesium glycinate supports estrogen clearance alongside its well-documented benefits for mood, sleep and PMS symptoms.

Adequate dietary fiber. Estrogen that has been conjugated by the liver is excreted via bile into the gut. Here, it should bind to fiber and be eliminated in stool. Without adequate fiber, beta-glucuronidase activity deconjugates the estrogen, allowing it to be reabsorbed rather than excreted. 25 to 35g of dietary fiber daily — from vegetables, legumes, oats and seeds — supports estrogen excretion directly.

Reducing alcohol. Even moderate alcohol consumption measurably elevates circulating estrogen. In an estrogen-dominant state, alcohol is the single most impactful dietary change. Reducing or eliminating alcohol — particularly in the luteal phase when the estrogen-progesterone ratio is already under pressure — produces measurable improvements within one to two cycles.

Managing cortisol. Reducing the cortisol load that competes with progesterone — through cycle-aware training, consistent sleep, stress reduction and magnesium — functionally improves progesterone's ability to balance estrogen without changing estrogen levels at all.

How do you reduce estrogen dominance naturally?
Cruciferous vegetables 3 to 5 times per week for DIM-supported liver estrogen metabolism. Magnesium glycinate 375mg daily for phase two liver detoxification. 25 to 35g dietary fiber daily for estrogen excretion. Reducing or eliminating alcohol — the single most impactful dietary change. Managing cortisol load through cycle-aware training and sleep protection. These address the specific mechanisms of estrogen dominance rather than its symptoms.
