The Best Workout for
Each Phase of Your Menstrual Cycle.

The same workout does not serve you equally across all four phases of your cycle. The hormonal environment changes every week — and the training that is most productive changes with it. Here is the complete phase-by-phase training guide based on the actual science of female exercise physiology.

By Andreea Mighiu  ·  Zōē Women  ·  Hormonal health education

Menstrual phase workouts — days 1 to 5

The menstrual phase is the most consistently mismanaged phase in women's training. Most training programmes — including many "cycle syncing" programmes — still recommend some form of performance training during menstruation. The physiological case for rest during this phase is stronger than the case for performance training.

Estrogen and progesterone are at their monthly lowest. Iron is being actively lost through blood loss. The body's physiological priority is cellular cleanup and hormonal reset — not performance output. High-intensity training during menstruation adds cortisol to the most depleted hormonal environment of the month.

What works: Walking 20 to 30 minutes daily. Gentle yoga focusing on hip opening and forward folds. Light stretching. Breathing work. This is not a fitness loss — it is strategic rest that makes the follicular phase training more productive.

What to prioritize nutritionally: Iron-rich foods at every meal — red meat, lentils, dark leafy greens, pumpkin seeds. Vitamin C alongside iron for absorption. Anti-inflammatory foods — fatty fish, ginger, turmeric — to manage prostaglandin-driven discomfort.

Should I work out during my period?
Light movement — gentle walking, yoga, stretching — is generally beneficial and may reduce cramp severity through blood flow support. Heavy resistance training or HIIT during menstruation is not recommended. Estrogen and progesterone are at their lowest, iron is being depleted, and the body's physiological priority is reset rather than performance. Rest is appropriate and strategic — not weakness.

Follicular and ovulatory phase workouts — days 6 to 16

This is your performance window. Ten to twelve days per cycle when the hormonal environment most strongly supports maximum training output, fastest recovery and best adaptation to a progressive stimulus.

Follicular phase (Days 6 to 13) — the building phase.

Resistance training: Heavy compound movements — back squat, Romanian deadlift, bench press, bent-over row, overhead press. Four to five sets per movement. Progressive overload attempts — this is the week to attempt weight increases. Rep ranges: 4 to 8 for strength focus, 8 to 12 for hypertrophy focus. Three to four sessions per week.

Cardio: High-intensity interval training once per week. Longer moderate-intensity cardio sessions as complement to resistance. Running, cycling, rowing — at higher intensities than you would manage in the luteal phase.

Ovulatory phase (Days 14 to 16) — peak performance.

Everything that applies in the follicular phase applies here with additional capacity. Testosterone peak produces higher pain tolerance, faster reaction time and stronger neuromuscular recruitment. Add volume to existing programmes — more sets, more reps at the same weight. Attempt any exercises or weights you have been building toward. Three days of your peak monthly physical capacity.

When is the best time to exercise for weight loss as a woman?
The follicular phase — days 6 to 13. Insulin sensitivity is highest, fat mobilization is most supported and cortisol sensitivity is lowest. High-intensity resistance training and moderate cardio in this phase produce the most favorable hormonal conditions for fat loss. The same effort in the late luteal phase — when cortisol sensitivity peaks and insulin sensitivity is reduced — produces significantly less fat loss return.

Luteal phase workouts — days 17 to 28

The luteal phase is the most nuanced training phase and the one where most women make the most consequential training errors — either pushing too hard through the late luteal phase and worsening hormonal symptoms, or using the phase as an excuse to reduce training excessively in the early luteal window where productive work is still very much available.

Early luteal phase (Days 17 to 22) — moderate productive training.

Resistance training: Three to four sets per compound movement at 70 to 80 percent of follicular phase weights. Technique-focused work — this is an excellent window for perfecting form on complex movements. Functional and accessory work. Three sessions per week.

Cardio: Moderate intensity — heart rate 130 to 150 bpm. Pilates, cycling, swimming. Avoid HIIT in this window.

Late luteal phase (Days 23 to 28) — restore and protect.

Resistance training: Two sessions maximum. Weights at 50 to 60 percent of follicular phase. Focus on range of motion, breathing and form quality. No new weight attempts. No maximum effort.

Cardio: Walking. Gentle yoga. Nothing that significantly elevates heart rate or cortisol. This is the most important recovery phase of the month — what you do here determines the quality of the next follicular phase training window.

What is the best workout for the luteal phase?
Early luteal — moderate resistance training at 70 to 80 percent intensity, moderate cardio. Late luteal — light resistance, low-intensity movement, walking, gentle yoga. Protecting sleep and cortisol management matter more than training output in the late luteal phase. The recovery quality of this phase directly determines the productivity of the following follicular phase.
What exercise should I do during each phase of my menstrual cycle?
Menstrual — gentle walking, yoga, no resistance training. Follicular — heavy compound resistance, HIIT, progressive overload. Ovulatory — maximum intensity, add volume, personal bests. Early luteal — moderate resistance, moderate cardio. Late luteal — light resistance, walking, yoga. This phase-specific framework produces consistently better outcomes than uniform training intensity across all four phases.
Is HIIT good for women with hormonal issues?
HIIT in the follicular and ovulatory phases is appropriate for most women. HIIT in the late luteal phase consistently worsens hormonal symptoms — cortisol sensitivity is highest and the additional cortisol from HIIT in this window reliably worsens PMS, disrupts sleep and suppresses the HPG axis over time. For women with PCOS or significant hormonal disruption, replacing late luteal HIIT with moderate resistance training and walking is one of the most effective single training adjustments.
