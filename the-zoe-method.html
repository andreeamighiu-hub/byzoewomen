Why Physiology Changes the Way You Train

Your physiology isn't
static.

Throughout the menstrual cycle, hormones such as estrogen and progesterone naturally fluctuate, influencing far more than reproductive health. These natural changes can affect how your body produces energy, recovers, responds to stress, regulates appetite, and performs during exercise. The Zōē Method adapts your strategy to support these physiological changes, rather than asking your body to perform the same way every day.

01 · Energy Production

Your body doesn't always fuel itself the same way.

Throughout the cycle, your body naturally shifts how it uses carbohydrates and fats for energy. These changes can influence training intensity, endurance, and how demanding exercise feels. The Zōē Method adapts training and nutrition to support changing energy demands.

02 · Recovery & Adaptation

Recovery isn't the same every week.

Hormonal fluctuations can influence muscle protein synthesis, connective tissue, and the time your body needs to recover between training sessions. Instead of repeating the same workload, your strategy evolves with your recovery capacity.

03 · Stress Response

Training is stress. The response isn't always the same.

Exercise is a healthy stressor, but the body's response to it can vary across the cycle. At certain times, the same workout may feel more demanding or require greater recovery. The goal isn't to train less. It's to apply the right stimulus at the right time.

04 · Body Composition

Progress is more than the number on the scale.

Hormonal fluctuations can temporarily influence water balance, appetite, and body weight. Understanding these natural changes helps prevent unnecessary frustration and keeps progress in perspective. The Zōē Method helps you interpret your body's signals rather than react to them.

05 · Performance

Performance isn't always linear.

Strength, motivation, coordination, and perceived effort naturally fluctuate throughout the cycle. Expecting peak performance every day can create unnecessary frustration. Your training adapts to your physiology, not the other way around.

06 · Brain & Focus

Your hormones influence more than your workouts.

Hormonal changes can also affect concentration, mood, sleep quality, motivation, and mental energy. Supporting performance means supporting the whole person, not just the training session. Because feeling better isn't only physical.

Common Questions

Questions about the
Zōē Method.

What is cycle-syncing training?

It's training with your hormones instead of around them. Instead of the same plan every single day, your training, nutrition, and recovery shift across the cycle's four phases (menstrual, follicular, ovulatory, luteal) to match what your body actually has to give that week.

Does training around your menstrual cycle actually work?

It doesn't mean training less. It means training at the right time, the right way. Estrogen and progesterone shift your energy, recovery, and stress response throughout the month. Work with that instead of against it, and consistency stops feeling like a fight.

What are the four phases of the menstrual cycle for training purposes?

Menstrual, follicular, ovulatory, and luteal. Each one brings its own mix of estrogen and progesterone, which is exactly why your energy, recovery, and performance don't feel the same every single day.

Is the Zōē Method only for women who track their natural cycle?

Not at all. Whether you're on hormonal birth control, your cycle is irregular, or you track it closely, the Zōē Method is built around how your body actually functions right now, not a textbook version of it.

Is the Zōē Method based on real exercise science?

Yes. It's grounded in real exercise physiology and female-specific sports science, shaped by conversations with endocrinologists and healthcare professionals, and paired with Francesco's 18+ years of hands-on coaching. This isn't a fixed program, it's a methodology that adapts to you.
